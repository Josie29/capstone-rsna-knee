# The 12 Knee Abnormalities

One-pager on each binary label: what it is, what it looks like on MRI, and the best plane to see it. See [anatomical-planes.md](anatomical-planes.md) for plane orientation and [fluid-and-fat-sensitivity.md](fluid-and-fat-sensitivity.md) for which sequence type each finding shows up on.

## Quick reference

| Label | What it is | Key MRI sign | Best plane |
|---|---|---|---|
| ACL | Torn anterior cruciate ligament | Fiber discontinuity, bright T2 signal, abnormal slope | Sagittal |
| MCL | Torn/sprained medial collateral ligament | Edema/bright signal along inner-side ligament | Coronal |
| Medial Meniscus | Tear in inner-side cartilage pad | Bright line inside dark meniscus reaching surface | Sagittal + coronal |
| Lateral Meniscus | Tear in outer-side cartilage pad | Same as medial, outer side | Sagittal + coronal |
| Medial OA | Wear-and-tear arthritis, inner femur-tibia compartment | Cartilage loss, osteophytes, marrow lesions | Coronal |
| Lateral OA | Same, outer femur-tibia compartment | Same, outer side | Coronal |
| PF OA | Same, kneecap-femur joint | Same, behind patella | Axial |
| Effusion | Excess fluid in the joint | Bright T2 fluid distending the capsule | Sagittal/axial |
| Synovitis | Inflamed joint lining | Thickened synovium, signal in Hoffa's fat pad | Sagittal/axial |
| Baker's | Fluid-filled cyst behind the knee | Bright T2 pouch in popliteal fossa | Axial/sagittal |
| Contusion | Bone bruise (marrow injury, no break) | Fuzzy marrow edema, no fracture line | All (fat-sat) |
| Fracture | Actual break in bone | Discrete dark line + cortical break | All |

## Details

### Ligament injuries

**ACL (anterior cruciate ligament)** — central ligament running obliquely front-to-back inside the joint; stops the tibia sliding forward. Classic sports injury (pivot/twist). MRI: torn fibers look discontinuous, swollen, and bright on fluid-sensitive sequences; the ligament may sag or be absent from its notch ("empty notch sign"). Strong tell: ~70% of ACL tears come with a characteristic bone-bruise pair (lateral femoral condyle + posterior lateral tibia) — so ACL and Contusion labels will co-occur often.

**MCL (medial collateral ligament)** — flat ligament down the inner side of the knee; resists the knee bending inward. Injured by a blow to the outer knee. MRI: fluid/edema tracking along the ligament (grade 1) up to full fiber disruption (grade 3). Runs vertically along the side, so coronal shows its whole length.

### Meniscal tears

**Medial / Lateral Meniscus** — two C-shaped fibrocartilage shock absorbers sitting on the tibia. Healthy meniscus is uniformly black on all sequences (a "bow-tie" in sagittal, triangles in coronal). A tear is a bright line cutting through the black that reaches an articular surface. Medial tears are more common (medial meniscus is more anchored); medial posterior-horn tears frequently accompany ACL tears. Degenerative tears accompany OA.

### Osteoarthritis (three compartments)

The knee has three compartments, each with its own OA label:
- **Medial OA** — inner femur-on-tibia surface (most commonly affected; bears most load)
- **Lateral OA** — outer femur-on-tibia surface
- **PF OA** — patellofemoral: kneecap gliding on the front of the femur

Same disease, different location: cartilage thinning/defects, bony spurs (osteophytes) at joint margins, subchondral bone marrow lesions and cysts, often with meniscal degeneration and extrusion. Tibiofemoral compartments are best judged on coronal; the patellofemoral joint on axial. OA labels correlate with each other and with meniscus/effusion/synovitis labels.

### Fluid and inflammation

**Effusion** — too much synovial fluid in the joint, a nonspecific response to almost any injury or arthritis. MRI: fluid is bright on T2 — look for it pooling above the patella (suprapatellar recess) and distending the capsule.

**Synovitis** — inflammation of the synovium (the joint's lining membrane) itself. MRI without contrast: synovial thickening and irregular signal, often inferred from signal in Hoffa's (infrapatellar) fat pad. Travels with effusion and OA — expect heavy label co-occurrence.

**Baker's cyst** (popliteal cyst) — joint fluid herniating out the back of the knee through a weak spot between the medial gastrocnemius and semimembranosus tendons. MRI: a well-defined bright-T2 fluid pouch behind the medial knee, connected to the joint by a thin neck. Usually secondary to effusion/OA/meniscal pathology.

### Bone injuries

**Contusion (bone bruise)** — trabecular microfracture + bleeding/edema inside the bone, cortex intact. Invisible on X-ray. MRI: ill-defined marrow region that is dark on T1, bright on T2/fat-suppressed sequences. Its *pattern* is a footprint of the injury mechanism (e.g., the ACL "kissing" bruise pair).

**Fracture** — an actual break: discrete low-signal fracture line through the marrow and/or a cortical step/break, with surrounding edema. Distinction from contusion = presence of a defined line/cortical disruption; the edema around a fracture looks just like a contusion.

## Label relationships worth knowing

- Trauma cluster: ACL ↔ Contusion ↔ MCL ↔ medial meniscus (one injury event, multiple labels).
- Degenerative cluster: OA (any compartment) ↔ meniscus tears ↔ effusion ↔ synovitis ↔ Baker's.
- Effusion is near-universal companion pathology — expect it to be the most frequent positive label.
