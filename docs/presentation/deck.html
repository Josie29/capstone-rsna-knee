The task

One knee MRI study in, 12 probabilities out

  • Input: a multi-planar MRI study — sagittal, coronal, axial; fluid-sensitive and non-fluid sequences (~5.5 series per study)
  • Output: per-study probability for 12 binary findings — ACL, MCL, both menisci, 3 osteoarthritis compartments, effusion, synovitis, Baker's cyst, contusion, fracture
  • Scored by macro-averaged ROC AUC — every finding counts equally, rare or common
  • Delivered as a Kaggle notebook — no internet, 9-hour runtime cap

The catch

Only 58 of 4,407 studies are labeled

  • The other 98.7% carry only a free-text radiology report — in ~a dozen languages across ~20 countries
  • The report does not exist at test time — text is a training tool, not an input
  • So the real task: mine labels from reports, then train an imaging model on what you mined
  • The 58 gold studies are the only ground truth to validate the mining — far too few to steer model selection alone

The plan

Four stages, mined labels first

STAGE 01

Mine

Multilingual report → 12 pseudo-labels per study, validated against the 58 gold studies.

STAGE 02

Select

Pick the right series per study — 6 types (3 planes × fluid / non-fluid) — decode and normalize DICOM.

STAGE 03

Train

Pretrained 2D backbones per plane, fused to study-level predictions, trained on pseudo-labels.

STAGE 04

Submit

Offline inference notebook, weights attached as datasets, budgeted under 9 hours.

Sequencing insight: mining quality sets the ceiling on everything downstream — and it can be built and iterated without touching a single DICOM.

Stage 01 · Mine

The ceiling-setter: reports become labels

  • A multilingual encoder reads each report and emits 12 binary pseudo-labels
  • Rejected — regex/keyword rules: ~12 languages × 12 findings × negation handling is brittle beyond a sanity pass
  • Rejected — translate-then-English-model: adds a failure stage; multilingual encoders skip it
  • Validation: per-finding agreement with the 58 gold studies
  • Every point of label noise here is noise the imaging model trains on

Stages 02–03 · Select & Train

From 247 GiB of DICOM to a model

  • Series metadata collapses to 6 types — 3 planes × fluid-sensitive / non-fluid. Every study has all 3 planes, so selection is the decision, not availability
  • Mixed compression (JPEG Lossless, JPEG 2000, uncompressed) — half the data won't decode without the right handlers
  • Per-plane 2D backbones over slices → series aggregate → plane fusion → 12 study-level probabilities
  • Trained on Kaggle GPUs with the data pre-mounted — skipping the 247 GiB download entirely

Stage 04 · Submit

The 9-hour box

  • No internet: code and weights ship as Kaggle datasets attached to the notebook
  • ~1,300 studies × ~30 slices × several series must fit in 9 hours — inference cost was budgeted before choosing an architecture
  • Efficiency track: a second prize pool scores AUC per unit runtime — being fast is itself a leaderboard
  • The repo is the source of truth — notebooks are thin wrappers over src/knee/, deployed by API, never edited in the web UI

Why this matters

Radiologist time is the scarcest input in medical AI

  • Expert annotation is the bottleneck — labeling thousands of MRI studies by hand doesn't scale
  • But hospitals already hold millions of studies with reports attached — weak supervision from reports is how those archives get unlocked
  • Multilingual mining is the realistic version of the problem: real archives span languages and sites (this dataset: 22 sites, ~20 countries)
  • The recipe generalizes to any paired image + report modality — chest X-ray, CT, pathology

What's next

Extensions on the table

  • Better miner: LLM-based labeling vs local encoder (pending a rules check on sending report text to external APIs)
  • Self-training loop: retrain miner and imaging model on each other's confident predictions
  • 3D architectures: does true volumetric context pay for its runtime?
  • Per-finding calibration: macro AUC rewards ranking each finding well independently
  • Efficiency track: distillation and pruning for the runtime-scored prize pool
